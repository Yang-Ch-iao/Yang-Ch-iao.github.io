ENTERPRISE BENCHMARKS

Reasoning over evolving enterprise knowledge

Answering a question inside a company can require piecing together conversations, documents, and decisions that change over time. EnterpriseSaga creates a benchmark for this challenge by simulating one coherent company history across 12 systems. Shared business activities generate consistent records; questions are then built with verified evidence across those records.

I led the simulation, question construction, retrieval pipelines, and agent evaluation. The benchmark contains 1.213 billion tokens and 966 questions; the pipeline shows how the company history and its questions are built together.

MOLECULAR OPTIMIZATION

World models for molecular optimization

Improving a molecule means finding useful structural changes in a vast chemical space. MolWorld organizes this search as a molecule-transfer graph: each node is a whole molecule, and each edge represents a small, verified structural transformation.

Our world model predicts how this graph can grow to guide molecular optimization. The animation shows the expansion step: five connected molecules provide a shared context for generating candidates, whose links are verified before they join the graph.

AI FOR NEUROSCIENCE

XAI-Refine

A model can predict brain age accurately while learning relationships that conflict with neuroscience. XAI-Refine extracts reliable claims from model explanations, then retrieves and verifies domain knowledge from scientific literature to check those claims.

The two sets of claims are compared within the same scope. Their discrepancy defines a minimal explanation revision, which becomes a new regularization term for retraining. An accepted model is explained again, closing the knowledge-guided correction loop.

With Emory Radiology and Neurology.

SEMANTIC RELATIONS

Relation-conditioned multimodal embeddings

The same image–text item can be related to different items for different reasons: a sun hat may belong with tennis gear or fishing gear. An embedding represents items as points, with shorter distances indicating greater similarity. A single fixed embedding has to serve all these relations.

RCML instead learns a space conditioned on a natural-language relation, using relation-conditioned projections and contrastive training. In the comparison, the products stay the same while RCML changes their distances for tennis versus fishing.

MULTIMODAL SARCASM DETECTION · AAAI 2023

MILNet

Sarcasm can arise from a mismatch between what a post says and what its image shows. Local object details and the overall scene reveal different clues, so relying on either view alone can miss the intended meaning.

MILNet models both views and links them through selective mutual learning: each branch teaches the other only on examples it predicts correctly. The diagram shows this exchange; the two cases below it illustrate how the views compensate for each other’s blind spots.
