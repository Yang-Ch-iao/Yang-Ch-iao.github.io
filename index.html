LLM Agents & Retrieval

EnterpriseSaga

Finding the right evidence as a company evolves

Company records rarely tell a complete story in one place. Answering a question may mean following evidence across systems and dates, while separating projects that share people and terminology. During my internship at Causal Dynamics Lab, I led EnterpriseSaga to study this problem at the scale of a billion-token company.

We simulate Clearpath, a B2B SaaS company with one shared history, then generate records across 12 systems from its projects and recurring operations. Question-conditioned augmentation adds similar activities that make the right evidence harder to identify. The framework shown here produces 966 multi-hop and synthesis questions with traceable support. I led benchmark construction, retrieval pipelines, and agent evaluation.

MolWorld

Learning how a molecular search space can grow

A useful molecular candidate should come with a path back to known compounds. We introduce a molecule-transfer graph: each node is a molecule, and each verified matched molecular pair (MMP) edge records a small structural change.

MolWorld learns a world model to predict future graph expansions. Shared dynamics and a property-specific scorer guide the search; a generator proposes molecules from a connected context, and verification adds candidates that connect back to the graph. The animation follows this process: select five connected anchors, generate a batch, verify its links, and grow the graph. This makes optimization a search over traceable molecular changes.

In collaboration with Merck & Co., Inc.

XAI-Refine

A brain-age model can predict accurately for reasons that lack neurobiological support. We use its explanations to ask scientific questions, retrieve domain literature, and compare model claims with verified neuroscience findings in the same explanatory space.

The mismatch becomes a minimal revision and a targeted training constraint. The key is to refine the same quantity that was explained: explanation, regularization, and validation retain the same variable and measurement operator, using a differentiable surrogate for training when needed. Only updates that improve the target explanation while preserving prediction quality and unrelated explanations return to the model. The loop then starts again.

With Emory Radiology and Neurology.

Multimodal Learning

RCML

The same item, a different relation

A sun hat might belong with a backpack when shopping for tennis, but with a rod when shopping for fishing. One fixed embedding per item cannot express both relationships through different distances.

We condition image–text representations on the natural-language relation. Relation embeddings project shared features into different spaces; relation-aware contrastive learning trains compatibility and selectivity. In the comparison, traditional embeddings stay fixed while RCML changes the same items’ positions and distances as the relation switches.

MILNet

Learning sarcasm from complementary views

Sarcasm can hinge on a small visual detail or on the whole scene. MILNet pairs local, semantic-guided reasoning over objects and text with global image–text attention, so details and context can inform each other.

Selective mutual learning lets each branch teach the other on examples it predicts correctly. The two cases show why both views matter: one needs whole-scene context, the other facial details. Local and global reasoning have different blind spots; learning together helps resolve them.
